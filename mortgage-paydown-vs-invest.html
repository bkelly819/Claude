From the client's email, and how it's modeled

"It's about $1,070,000 at 7% for a 30 year fixed. I am planning to pay an extra 2-3K a month to pay the mortgage off early."

Same dollars, same dates. Each month both options spend the same total: the scheduled payment plus the extra amount. Option A sends the extra to principal; Option B invests it in a taxable account. When either loan is paid off, the freed-up payment is invested (if that box is checked), so neither option gets more cash than the other.

Net worth = investment account minus the tax owed if sold, plus any uninvested cash, minus the remaining mortgage. The house is worth the same under both options, so it is left out.

Mortgage interest deduction. Each year's interest is multiplied by the share of the average balance under the limit. The benefit counts only the amount by which itemizing beats the standard deduction, at the marginal federal and state rates. State taxes deducted on the federal return (SALT) are not modeled. Standard deductions are held flat.

Investment taxes. The taxable yield is taxed each year at the federal qualified rate, plus NIIT and state tax if checked. Remaining gains are taxed at the end if "as if sold" is checked. Returns are steady, not variable; real markets will not be.

Not modeled: the planned $30–40K a year in renovations (that money is outside this comparison but competes for the same cash flow), refinance closing costs, PMI, inflation, and the liquidity value of having invested assets you can reach versus home equity you can't.

Missing details are left blank and flagged. This is an illustration based on stated assumptions, not a guarantee of future results.
